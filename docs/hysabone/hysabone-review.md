# HysabOne teardown: UI/UX and feature notes from the "Setting up HysabOne" video series

Researched 16 September 2026 by watching all 14 videos in the [HysabOne Software "Setting up HysabOne" playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLNwqFA4984plD2TXqWHAjrzsasdTDCW5L) **and** all 13 videos in the companion [HysabOne Software "Data Entry in HysabOne" playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLNwqFA4984pldmFFgxc8Huxxus8j_g7Ea), frame-by-frame (screenshots at seek points through each video), and cross-checking against hysabone.com. This is observational — read from a tutorial series and a marketing site, not from source code or a live trial account — so treat specifics (exact field validation, edge-case behaviour) as inferred, not verified. Where the demo data itself is informative (or clearly just seed/test data), that's called out. Screenshot image files backing this review (list-page, form, and dropdown captures from both playlists) are saved alongside this report in the `hysabone-screenshots/` folder.

**The short answer to "is it OSS packaged as commercial": no evidence of that.** HysabOne is proprietary software built by a Pakistani company, Good Tech (Pvt) Ltd, with a lineage back to 2013 custom software (see §1). More specifically, the app's own URLs (`go.hysabone.com/ords/r/app/hysabone/...`) are the signature pattern of an **Oracle APEX application served through Oracle REST Data Services (ORDS)** — a proprietary Oracle low-code stack, not an open-source framework. That's a direct observation from the browser address bar during playback, not a sourced claim; worth a quick independent confirmation (e.g. hitting `go.hysabone.com/ords/` directly) if it matters for a specific decision, but it's a strong signal on its own.

## 1. Company and commercial context

- **Vendor**: Good Tech (Pvt) Ltd, "Built by Pakistanis for Pakistani businesses." Product name HysabOne ("hysab" = account/reckoning in Urdu).
- **History**: started 2013 as bespoke accounting software for a single trading-business client → rebuilt 2017 → company incorporated 2021 → soft-launched as a multi-tenant cloud SaaS December 2023 → expanded to UAE and Saudi Arabia in 2024 → 500+ businesses claimed as of the About page.
- **Positioning**: "#1 Accounting & Inventory Software in Pakistan | Cloud ERP for SMEs."
- **Pricing** (from hysabone.com/pricing, PKR/month, billed monthly; ~12.5% off annually):

  14-day free trial, no setup fee, JazzCash/Easypaisa/card/bank transfer accepted. This is directly useful as a benchmark: it sits inside the Rs 2,000–7,000/month band the Pakistan-market research already put on the "paying, registered-SME tier" — see `pakistan-market.md` §1 — and it's **not yet in that doc's competitor table**, despite looking more established (self-reported 500+ customers, 3 regions, a 12-year product lineage) than most of the vendors already listed there.

## 2. Information architecture

**Left sidebar** (persistent, dark green, icon + label, collapsible sub-menus), in order: Dashboard, Daily Activity, Sales, Purchase, Expenses, Manufacturing, Inventory, Attendance, Accounts, Reports, Manage, Payroll, WhatsApp Help. That top-level list alone tells you the product's scope claim: this is not an accounting-only tool, it's a lightweight ERP — GL/AR/AP accounting, inventory/warehouse, light manufacturing (BOM-style consumption tracking, not full MRP), HR/attendance/payroll, and a chat-support channel, all in one nav rail.

**Top bar**: company/tenant switcher ("GreenLeaf Paper Traders (Pvt) Ltd. ▾" — confirms multi-company/multi-tenant per login), logged-in user with team name, and a small green **"Data" pill — a global quick-create menu of data-entry-form shortcuts**, reachable from any screen. The one instance glimpsed in these videos (a pre-transition "up next" frame, not deliberately opened) listed Sale Order, Payment Receipt, Purchase Order, Consumption, and Production — i.e. the highest-frequency day-to-day transaction types, not the master-data/setup forms this playlist otherwise covers. It's the same "let the user jump straight to the thing they do constantly" instinct as the per-record "Show On Dashboard?" flag, applied to transaction entry instead of reporting.

**"Manage Business"** is the real settings map — worth reproducing in full since it's the clearest single artifact of everything the product configures:

- **Master Data**: Taxes & Tax Groups, Item Categories, Items *(2,437 in the demo tenant)*, Customer *(65)*, Suppliers *(15)*, Sale Agents *(4)* — each with a live count badge.
- **HysabOne Business Account**: Business Profile, Preferences, Implementation Guide, Billing & Subscription, Image Storage, Terms & Conditions.
- **Accounts**: Expense Accounts, Liquid Accounts (Bank & Cash), Consumption Accounts, Internal Issue Accounts, Chart of Accounts, Accounting Period.
- **Geography**: Branches, Stores.
- **Users**: Users Management, User Activity Monitoring, Simple Access Log.
- **HR**: Employees, Biometric Devices, Departments, Designations, SOP.
- **Trash**: a soft-delete/recycle-bin area (seen for Account Vouchers at least) — deleted records aren't gone, they're recoverable.

Each row is a `>` chevron link into its own list+form pair. The consistent shape across nearly every module in the whole product is: **list page** (search box, an "Actions ▾" dropdown, sometimes a row-count/page-size selector, a green primary "Create/New X" button top right) → **modal or slide-in form** (labeled fields, required fields marked with `*`, Cancel + green primary submit button) → **toast** ("Row created" / "Action Processed", green, top-right, auto-dismissing) → list re-renders. That's the entire interaction grammar of the app; once you've seen it once you've seen it for all 14 setup areas.

## 3. Module-by-module notes

### Dashboard
Company name + welcome line, then a tab strip — **Short-Term Analysis | Overview | Agents Performance Analysis | Watchlists** — over a card grid: "Today" and "Yesterday" cards (Sales Revenue, Expense, Gross Profit, Orders, Staff Present/Absent, Net Cash Change, each with an "Open >" drill-in), and a "This Month" card with small bar-chart sparklines comparing this month vs last month for sales and expense. Small abbreviated stat badges (e.g. "OCT SALE / OCT PROFIT / OCT ORDERS") sit above the cards. Two things stand out: attendance ("Staff Present/Absent") is a first-class dashboard metric even before any accounting setup is done, and there's a dedicated **Agents Performance Analysis** tab — sales-agent/commission tracking is treated as core, not bolted on (see Sale Agents below). "Watchlists" wasn't opened in these videos but implies user-configurable metric pinning, which lines up with the "Show On Dashboard?" toggle that appears on almost every entity form (expense categories, customers, suppliers, items) — a consistent, product-wide pattern of "let the user promote any record to a dashboard widget."

### Business Profile
Single-company record: Business Name, Business Type (dropdown, e.g. "Retail"), Industry (dropdown), Email, Phone, Website, single-line Address, and a "Since" field (establishment date) — plus a repeatable **Contact Details** block (Name/Phone/Email) for key business contacts. Edit is a slide-in panel over a dimmed background; view mode mirrors the edit layout as a read-only tile grid.

### Branches & Regions
Two-level geographic hierarchy: **Regions** (name, optional parent region — so regions can nest, e.g. city-level regions under a head-office region — plus city and telephone) contain **Branches** (name, address, telephone, parent region). The product auto-creates a default "Head Office" region for every new company. This maps directly onto how Pakistani trading/distribution businesses actually operate (a head office plus city-level sales points), and it's the join point for Stores, Sale Agents, and (per the demo data) per-branch cash accounts.

### Stores
Each Region can hold multiple **Stores**, each typed by stock role: *Finished Goods Stock*, *Waste Stock*, *Raw Material Stock* in the observed data. That three-way split (raw material in → waste tracked separately → finished goods out) is a real manufacturing/production inventory model, not just "warehouse = location" — it implies goods-movement postings between store types as part of the Manufacturing module. Store creation form: Region (required), Store Name (required), Country, City.

### Chart of Accounts / typed account setup (Equity, Drawing, Liquid, Consumption, Internal Issue)
This is the most detailed part of the product observed, and the most relevant to PHP Ledger directly.

- **Account creation is unified**: one "Account Form" (Account Category e.g. EQUITY, Account Type e.g. Equity/Drawing, Description, Choose Parent Folder, Is Folder? Yes/No, Is Account Active/Disabled) is reused across Equity, Drawing, and general Chart-of-Accounts entry — only the category/type defaults differ per entry point. A nice, cheap touch: the form's right-hand panel shows a live mini preview of "How account types affect your reports" (a schematic P&L and Balance Sheet), so a non-accountant setting up EQUITY vs Drawing can see where it lands before saving. Worth adopting as a UX pattern regardless of anything else in this doc.
- **The shipped default chart of accounts is deep, numbered, and Pakistan-specific.** Observed nodes include decimal-numbered categories (`3.1.3 Other Income`, `3.4 Marketing & Selling Expenses`), letter-prefixed expense codes (`N- Donation And Charity`, `O- Crockery & Office Supplies`, `P- Lease Rentals`), and — notably — real local-bank/Islamic-finance line items: `Profit On Pls Account` → `Mcb, I-9, Islamabad` (PLS = Profit & Loss Sharing, the Islamic-banking savings-account term; MCB is a real Pakistani bank), plus `Bank Alfalah, Std, Mcb Etc.`, `Exchange Gain (Export)`, `Mark Up On Finance Leases`, `Donation And Charity`. Every leaf account also carries a long auto-generated numeric code (e.g. `0000003679`) alongside its human label. This is a concretely more Pakistan-tailored default COA than ERPNext ships (the ERPNext review already on file notes ERPNext has **no** Pakistan chart of accounts at all) — it's evidence the gap PHP Ledger identified as an opportunity is real and that at least one local competitor has already filled part of it.
- **Named-partner equity, directly supporting AOP/partnership structures.** The demo tenant has *Musa Equity* / *Musa Drawing* and *Zain Equity* / *Zain Drawing* — i.e. each business partner gets their own equity and drawing sub-accounts by name, created through the same generic Account Form. This is exactly the AOP (Association of Persons) pattern PHP Ledger is already targeting per the ERPNext review's roadmap notes, and HysabOne treats it as a day-one setup step, not an afterthought.
- **Liquid Accounts (Bank & Cash)**: its own list+form, separate from the general COA screen (Description, Account Nature [Bank/Cash], Status, Starting Balance). Demo data shows real Pakistani bank names (Habib Bank Ltd, UBL) with opening dates, and multiple **per-branch cash-in-hand accounts** ("Cash In Hand - Blue Area Display", "Cash In Hand Lahore") — i.e. cash is tracked per physical location, not pooled into one company-wide cash account.
- **Consumption Accounts** are distinct from **Expense Categories**: Expense Categories (Bank Fees & Charges, Legal & Professional Fees, Rent, Payroll/Staff Salaries, etc., each with a "Show On Dashboard?" flag) look like the ordinary AP/expense classification list; **Consumption Accounts** ("Consumption Of Boiler Fuel", "Consumption Of Raw Material") look like a manufacturing-specific concept — material or utility consumed in production, tracked separately from a purchased/billed expense.
- **Internal Issue Accounts**: a picker that lets you select existing accounts/items (search shown: "paper for admin" matching "Delievery Fuel", "Temp Exp") as eligible for "internal issue" — i.e. taking stock out of inventory for internal use (not a sale) and reclassifying its value to an expense/consumption account, rather than a straight stock-to-COGS sale posting. (Note: "Delievery" is misspelled consistently across the UI — a small but real polish gap, and possibly a fingerprint if you ever need to identify the codebase/vendor from a support ticket or screenshot elsewhere.)
- **Architectural point worth flagging for PHP Ledger, not necessarily copying**: customer and supplier records appear to materialize as **individual leaf accounts inside the Chart of Accounts tree** under parent folders like "Accounts Receivables (Customer)" and an inventory-value parent (each customer's detail page shows an `Acc ID` tying back to a COA node). That's a real chart-of-accounts entry per customer/supplier — one COA leaf for each of the 65 customers and 15 suppliers in the demo tenant — rather than a single Receivable/Payable control account with a party-keyed open-item sub-ledger (the pattern the ERPNext review recommends and that scales better as party count grows). If accurate, this is a concrete "avoid" data point: party-per-COA-node is simple to build and simple to understand in a small tree, but it's exactly the design ERPNext moved away from for performance reasons, and it's worth confirming PHP Ledger's AR/AP design (open-item ledger keyed by party, not a COA node per party) doesn't drift toward it as convenience features get added. **One mitigation observed on the Chart of Accounts list itself**: the "Accounts Receivable" and "Inventory" parent nodes carry explicit description text — *"Receivable Child Accounts are hidden for performance reasons. To see Receivable Balances for each Customer, go to the Receivables report"* (and the equivalent for Inventory/Stock) — i.e. the per-party leaf accounts exist but are deliberately hidden from the default COA tree view, with dedicated Receivables/Stock reports as the intended way to see aggregate or per-party balances. The COA list also has a system-level **"Is Locked"** column protecting core accounts (Bank, Accounts Receivable, Inventory, Suspense, Equity, Revaluation Reserves…) from being edited or deleted by an end user. So the underlying per-party-node design may still be the scaling concern noted above, but the UI at least works around the "unreadable giant tree" symptom rather than exposing it raw.

### Taxes & Tax Groups
Two-level tax model: individual **Taxes** (description + rate %, e.g. "GST 4.5%", "Import Tax 5%") get bundled into named **Tax Groups** ("Taxes on Laptops", "Taxes on Cars - 800cc") with a computed total rate and a "Collection Debug" section on the form (an exposed calculation-debug view, unusual to surface to end users — possibly a support/troubleshooting aid, possibly just left in from development). Tax Groups list a "No. of SKUs" style column, implying items reference a tax group rather than a flat rate — sensible for import-duty-plus-GST compounding, and for varying rates across categories as different as laptops vs vehicles in the same tenant.

### Item Categories
Simple flat category list (Description + item-count QTY), paginated ("Rows: 50"), with a totals row. The demo categories mix consumer electronics (Cameras, Laptops, Mobiles, Tablets, Watches) and vehicles (Sedan Cars, SUVs, Crossovers, Mini Cars) — almost certainly shared generic seed/demo data reused across all the tutorial videos rather than anything specific to "GreenLeaf Paper Traders," so don't read industry signal into these category names specifically (contrast with the Items module below, where paper-industry fields appear that are clearly part of the product schema, not just demo labels).

### Items (the deep module — 14.5-minute video, by far the longest in the series)
This is where the product's real complexity lives. The **Item Form** covers:
- Item Name, Category (dropdown over the full category list), Custom Model Code, Remarks (free text — used in the demo for roll-dimension specs like "1x172, 1x342, 2x300").
- **Pricing mode**: "Fixed Pricing" (a locked Sale Price) vs **"Flexible Prices (Invoice To Invoice)"** — i.e. price is decided per sale order rather than fixed on the item master. Explicit helper text: "Should the operator be able to change Sale Price when creating a Sale Order?"
- **Quantity Nature / Stock Nature**, a 5-way choice that's explicitly industry-labeled in the UI, not generic:
  1. *Simple* (Recommended for most use cases)
  2. *No Inventory Tracking* (For Services)
  3. *Multi-Level Inventory* (**For Paper Reels, Clothing Articles, Paint Buckets etc.**)
  4. *Serial Number Tracking* (For Electronics, Cars, Luxury Items etc.)
  5. *Batch Handling*
  Selecting Multi-Level Inventory exposes a 2nd-level unit (e.g. primary unit KG, secondary unit "Each" for roll count) and a **GSM field** (grams-per-square-metre, the paper-industry weight spec) — a concrete, purpose-built field for the paper-trading vertical the demo company (a paper trader) represents, not a generic custom-field mechanism. This is a genuinely well-thought-out piece of vertical tailoring: the same item master handles a service business, a general retailer, a paper merchant, a clothing/paint retailer needing roll or batch counts, and an electronics/auto dealer needing serials, by picking one of five named "stock nature" presets rather than exposing raw inventory-method configuration.
- **Costing Method**: "Average Weighted Cost" observed (dropdown implies other options exist, not shown/opened in the video).
- Active? and Show On Dashboard? toggles, consistent with every other entity form in the product.

### Customers
List page grouped/filterable, with a "(Non-Registered)" status badge on most demo rows — almost certainly meaning "not sales-tax registered," a distinction that matters directly for Pakistani input-tax-credit eligibility and matches this project's own FBR research. Generic walk-in customers appear to be represented as repeated "CASH SALE" rows rather than a single shared placeholder customer. The **Customer Form** (Name, Phone, Email, Address, City, Opening Balance, Show On Dashboard?, "Is Client Blocked?" Unblocked/Blocked) includes a real **credit-hold toggle** — the customer-side equivalent of the supplier-hold feature the ERPNext review already recommended PHP Ledger adopt. The **customer detail page** ("Client Info") is a proper 360° view: quick actions (+ Payment Receipt, + Sale Order, Edit, **"Merge Customer With…"**), a profile card showing the linked ledger `Acc ID`, and tabs for Ledger / Sales / Payments / Analysis. The Merge action is a genuine, easy-to-underrate data-quality feature (duplicate customer records are a universal SME-data problem) worth considering for PHP Ledger's own customer/supplier masters once they exist.

### Suppliers
Same shape as Customers, but the list is **grouped by city** with a subtotal balance per city group ("City: Islamabad" / "City: Lahore" / "City: Peshawar" …) rather than a flat table — a genuinely useful default view for a business paying suppliers across several cities. The **Supplier Form** adds a "Sales Tax No." field (supplier's own STRN, relevant to input-tax-credit documentation) and a separate "Visible" toggle in addition to "Show On Dashboard?" (soft-hide vs dashboard-promote are treated as two independent flags, not one). A supplier detail page exposes Ledger / Vendor's Items / **Itemised Payables (POSTED)** tabs — payables broken down at the invoice/item level, not just an aggregate balance. The list also includes a named "Suspense Supplier" ("Hello World") row — a built-in catch-all bucket for unclassified/miscellaneous purchases, similar in spirit to a suspense account.

### Sale Agents
Agent Code, Name, Address, Phone, Email, **Region Name**, Active — each agent is tied to a branch/region ("Sale Center" on the create form) and a City/Country. This is what feeds the dashboard's "Agents Performance Analysis" tab. Commission-based field sales reps assigned to a specific location are a first-class concept in the product, which fits the route-sales / distribution pattern common among Pakistani trading businesses more than it fits a typical small-shop SaaS accounting tool.

## 4. Data Entry playlist — transactional UX (13 videos)

The playlist covered above is a setup/master-data series. HysabOne Software's channel has a second, parallel playlist for day-to-day transaction entry — [Data Entry in HysabOne](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLNwqFA4984pldmFFgxc8Huxxus8j_g7Ea), 13 short videos (~1.5–14 min each, ~66 min total) — watched the same way (seek-point screenshots per video). It confirms and extends nearly everything in §3; the standout new finding is that **transactional forms use a different, heavier UI pattern than the settings forms**, which is worth calling out on its own before the individual-module notes.

### Purchases, Sales, Sale Returns — the transaction-list pattern
Purchases, Sale Orders, Purchase Returns and Sale Returns all share one list shape: rows grouped under a date header ("Purchase/Sale/Return Date: Monday, 30 September, 2024"), each group showing a `Count: N` line, with per-row action buttons inline in the list itself — e.g. a green **"Receive Payment"** pill directly on each unpaid/partially-paid Sale Order row, no drill-in required first. A "Sale Terms" column shows Paid/Partial/Credit at a glance. The Sales module is organised as four tabs under one sidebar entry — **Sale Orders | Payment Receipts | Sale Returns | Customers** — rather than four separate nav links.

### The transaction-entry form is a different UI pattern from settings forms
Every settings/master-data form in §3 is a single modal or slide-in with flat fields. Multi-line transactions (Purchases, Production) instead open as a **dedicated full page**, split into an item-picker panel (search/choose item, qty, rate, tax) on one side and a running **Items** list on the other (with a "No item added in Purchase document!" empty state), plus separate Total/Discount and Payment/Balance/Status sections further down the page ("Sub-Total Amount — Includes item-level discounts"). This is the standard grid/builder pattern any multi-line invoice or bill needs, and it's materially heavier (page-level, not modal) than the CRUD-form pattern the rest of this review documents — worth designing as its own UI mode in PHP Ledger from the start, not as an extension of the master-data modal pattern.

### Reel/roll-level stock picker for Multi-Level Inventory items
Selling a paper item with the "Multi-Level Inventory" stock nature (§3 Items) surfaces a dedicated picker at the line-item level: a table of **KG-Wise Qty / No. of Reels In-Stock / Sale Qty**, letting the operator allocate a sale across specific reels/rolls instead of just entering a total quantity. This is the concrete transactional payoff of the 5-way stock-nature setting documented earlier — it's a real, worked-through feature, not a config option that dead-ends.

### Manufacturing: Consumption and Production are linked, and Production feeds a shared "Receivings" ledger
- **Consumption** ("View and manage raw material consumption from inventory") lists Issue ID / Issued From Store / Item Description / Quantity / Issue Worth / **Issued Against**, grouped by date — "Issued Against" ties a raw-material consumption entry back to the production job it was consumed for.
- **Production** entry uses the same item-picker-plus-running-list pattern as Purchases. Completing a production run doesn't just post a manufacturing journal entry — it creates an ordinary-looking **"Receiving" document** (observed: "Receiving #202124110100003") in the Inventory module, carrying a **Receiving Type: PRODUCTION** field, targeted at a specific store ("FG WARE HOUSE" — finished-goods warehouse), with the usual Item / Custom Model Code / Qty Details / Quantity / Received-At-Price / Item Worth line grid. In other words, **Purchases and Production both write into one shared "Receivings" ledger, discriminated by a Receiving Type field**, rather than separate goods-receipt tables per source. That's a clean, worth-copying data-model idea for PHP Ledger's own goods-receipt design: one inbound-inventory-movement document type tagged by source (Purchase / Production / Transfer-in / …), instead of N parallel tables.

### Bank/Cash Transfer and Shareholder Drawing are dashboard quick-actions, not sidebar pages
Both of these videos are short (under two minutes) because both flows open as a modal straight from the Dashboard's "Data" quick-create menu (§2), not navigated to via the sidebar:
- **"Transfer Money Between Liquid Accounts"**: Transfer From / Received Into, both dropdowns over the full liquid-accounts list.
- **"Drawing Form"**: Drawing Account (same liquid-accounts dropdown) + free-text Description, debiting a named partner's Drawing account (Musa Drawing, Zain Drawing — §3 Chart of Accounts).

Both dropdowns show the **full liquid-accounts list with live balances inline in the picker itself** — e.g. "CASH IN HAND (Bal: 192,197)", "CASH IN HAND - BLUE AREA DISPLAY (Bal: 4,898,140)", "MEEZAN BANK — MUSA (Bal: 1,4xx,xxx)", "MCB — SADDAR RWP (Bal: 272,150)", "ZAIN — BANK ALFALAH — xxxx636 (Bal: 0)". Two things worth noting: balances are shown right inside the selection dropdown (no need to leave the form to check a balance first), and bank accounts are named per person/branch/purpose rather than "Bank Account 1" — Meezan Bank is a well-known Pakistani Islamic bank, reinforcing the PLS/Islamic-banking chart-of-accounts entries already noted in §3.

### Manual Vouchers: free-text account search, plus a three-way approval/audit control on every voucher
The "Enter Voucher" form (Voucher Type: JV = Journal Voucher, plus BP/JP types glimpsed in the ledger list — likely Bank Payment / Journal Payment or similar) has a **typeahead "Search Account" field searching the entire Chart of Accounts by name** (typing "m" surfaces "mamu", "meeza", "musa" — informal partial matches against named partner/bank accounts), not a nested category picker. More significant: the **"Acc. Vouchers" ledger list carries three independent per-voucher control columns — Lock Status, Director Check?, and Audit Check?** — alongside the usual Voucher No / Type / Status / Parent Document ID / Amount / Created / User fields. That's a genuine maker-checker / segregation-of-duties control built into the general ledger at the voucher level, not just a status flag — relevant to PHP Ledger given that SMB accounting software is often the *only* internal control an SMB has.

### Expenses: a spreadsheet-style multi-line entry, not a single-expense form
The "Expense Form" is scoped by **Amount Spent From** (a liquid account) and **Region**, then presents an editable grid (Expense Category / Description / Amount, with Add Row / Delete Row / Actions / Edit / search-all-columns, and a "Basic Mode" toggle implying a simpler single-line alternative) rather than one category-plus-amount per form submission — closer to a small spreadsheet than a typical "add expense" dialog. This lets an operator log several different expense categories paid from the same cash/bank source and region in one sitting (e.g. fuel + tea + stationery from one trip to the market), which matches how SME petty-cash spending actually happens.

## 5. UI/UX patterns worth naming explicitly

- **List → modal/slide-in form → toast → refreshed list** is the single interaction pattern for nearly the whole app. Consistency here is a real strength; a new user who's created one entity type has effectively learned how to create all of them.
- **"Show On Dashboard?" as a universal per-record flag** (seen on expense categories, items, customers, suppliers) is a cheap, consistent way to let users build their own dashboard without a separate widget-configuration screen.
- **A global "Data" quick-create menu in the top bar**, present on every screen, jumping straight into the transaction forms used constantly (Sale Order, Payment Receipt, Purchase Order, Consumption, Production) rather than requiring nav-rail drilldown each time. Pairs naturally with the per-record dashboard-promotion flag above: one mechanism surfaces frequently-*read* things, the other surfaces frequently-*written* things, both reachable from anywhere.
- **In-context education at the point of decision** — the live P&L/Balance-Sheet preview on the Account Form, and the plain-language stock-nature descriptions ("For Paper Reels, Clothing Articles, Paint Buckets etc.") instead of raw enum values — repeatedly substitutes for a help page. This is the single most exportable idea in this whole review, independent of anything backend/architectural: explain the consequence of a choice right where the choice is made, in the business owner's language, not the accountant's.
- **Two independent visibility flags** (Active/Disabled, and Show-on-Dashboard, and on Suppliers a third "Visible" flag) rather than one soft-delete flag — lets a record be deactivated, hidden from a dashboard, and hidden from pickers, independently.
- **A Trash area** for at least some entities (seen for Account Vouchers) — soft-delete with recovery, consistent with the "never silently destroy an accounting record" instinct PHP Ledger's own immutability decisions already lean toward.
- **Grouping list views by a meaningful dimension** (Suppliers by city) rather than always defaulting to a flat table — worth remembering as a low-cost, high-value pattern once PHP Ledger has enough parties to make flat lists unwieldy.
- **Green-on-dark-teal branding**, card-based dashboard, breadcrumb trail (`Manage Business \ Suppliers`) on every settings sub-page, search + "Actions ▾" dropdown as the standard list-page toolbar. Visually closer to a modern SaaS admin (shadcn/ui-style cards, soft shadows, pill buttons) than to a legacy ERP grid — notable given the underlying stack is (apparently) Oracle APEX, which does not look like this out of the box; someone invested real effort in a custom theme.
- **Rough edges observed**: the "Delievery Fuel" / "Delievery" typo recurs across at least two different setup videos (Internal Issue Accounts and item search), suggesting it's baked into seed data or even the schema, not a one-off; an exposed "Collection Debug" panel on the Tax Group form suggests some dev/debug affordances haven't been fully hidden from the production UI.

## 6. What this means for PHP Ledger

- **Add HysabOne to the Pakistan competitor set.** It's more substantial than most of what's currently in `pakistan-market.md`'s local-competitor table (12-year lineage, 500+ businesses, 3-country footprint, documented 3-tier pricing) and it isn't listed there yet. A row has been added — see that file.
- **The Pakistan-specific chart of accounts gap is real but partially filled.** HysabOne's default COA (PLS accounts, named local banks, decimal/letter numbering, donation/charity line items) is concrete evidence that a generic COA is a genuine local pain point worth solving — and a competitive bar to clear, not just a hypothesis from the ERPNext review.
- **AOP/partnership equity structure (named partner Equity + Drawing pairs) is table stakes**, not a nice-to-have — a direct competitor ships it as a day-one setup step.
- **Contextual, plain-language help at the decision point** (the P&L/Balance-Sheet preview; the "For Paper Reels, Clothing Articles…" stock-nature descriptions) is cheap to build and is the most directly reusable idea here, independent of stack or architecture.
- **Watch the party-as-COA-node pattern as a cautionary data point**, not a model to copy — it's simple until the party count grows, which is exactly the scaling problem the ERPNext review already flagged (open-item ledger keyed by party > one GL row per party).
- **Credit-hold on customers (mirroring the supplier-hold idea already on file), a Merge-duplicate-party action, and Itemised/invoice-level payables (not just an aggregate balance)** are three concrete, small AR/AP features worth adding to the backlog for whenever PHP Ledger's AR/AP milestone starts.
- **A five-way, plain-language "stock nature" picker** (Simple / No tracking / Multi-level / Serial / Batch, each captioned with example industries) is a strong reference pattern for whenever PHP Ledger's own inventory milestone comes up — it's a good way to hide FIFO/serial/batch/multi-unit complexity behind an industry-recognisable label instead of an accounting term.
- **One inbound-inventory-movement ledger discriminated by a type field** (Purchases and Production both post into a shared "Receivings" list, tagged by Receiving Type) is a clean data-model pattern worth matching for PHP Ledger's own goods-receipt design, instead of separate tables per source (§4).
- **Maker-checker controls at the voucher level** (Lock Status / Director Check? / Audit Check? on every manual journal voucher, §4) is a concrete, buildable feature for PHP Ledger's own manual-journal-entry screen — segregation of duties matters even for a 3-person SMB back office, and it's cheap to bolt onto a voucher list as status columns.
- **Show live account balances inside the account-picker dropdown itself** (seen on the bank/cash-transfer and drawing quick-actions, §4) is a small, cheap UX win worth copying: no need to leave the transfer/payment form to go check a balance first.
- **Plan for a second, heavier "transaction grid" UI mode from the start**, not as a retrofit of the settings-form modal pattern — multi-line transactions (purchases, production, multi-category expense entry) all need an item/line-grid builder, which is structurally different from the single-record modal that works fine for master data (§4).

## 7. Caveats and things not verified

- This is read from a tutorial series with demo/seed data (item categories mixing electronics and vehicles, large/odd balances, a "TestingGreat" expense category) — treat specific numbers and some category choices as artifacts of a shared demo tenant, not real customer data or necessarily permanent product behaviour.
- The Oracle APEX/ORDS conclusion is a direct read of the URL pattern shown during screen recording (`go.hysabone.com/ords/r/app/hysabone/...`), not a sourced claim from the vendor — high confidence given how distinctive that URL shape is to ORDS, but worth a five-second confirmation if it ever matters for a decision.
- Both the setup and the transactional (Data Entry) playlists are now reviewed, but some areas are still only glimpsed in passing rather than fully walked through: Payroll, Attendance/biometric integration, the native mobile apps, and WhatsApp e-invoicing were never opened (sidebar labels and one dashboard tab only) — treat anything said about those as absent, not as "reviewed and found simple."
- The Data Entry playlist's screenshots were taken at fixed seek points (10%/20%/30%… into each video) rather than by watching every second, so some UI states shown briefly by the presenter (e.g. validation errors, edge-case fields) may not be captured even though the general shape of each form is.
- Where a video's playback stalled and a seek point landed on a loading spinner rather than settled UI, that seek point was skipped rather than force-captured — coverage per video is representative, not necessarily one frame per named feature.
