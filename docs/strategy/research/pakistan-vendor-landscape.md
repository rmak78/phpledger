# Research: Pakistan SME accounting, POS and ERP vendors

Research date: 15 September 2026. Supporting evidence for [Pakistan market](../PAKISTAN-MARKET.md). Vendor claims are reported as claims; anything not confirmed by a primary or reputable secondary source is marked UNVERIFIED. Prices are as published on the cited date.

## 1. Regulatory backdrop that shapes every buying decision

- **FBR POS integration (Tier-1 retailers):** FBR data reported 16,082 registered Tier-1 branches with only 1,541 "connected" and 12,851 (80%) "disconnected" — roughly 9.6% compliance (Feb 2026, Business Recorder / Profit). Separately FBR claims ~35,953 "integrated" outlets (Apr 2026); the two figures measure different things (ever-integrated vs currently transmitting). Consumers pay Re 1 per invoice POS fee; ~Rs 1.55bn collected since Aug 2021.
- **FBR Digital Invoicing (sales-tax e-invoicing):** SRO 1852(I)/2025 phased all sales-tax registered persons in by 31 Dec 2025 (last phase); SRO 288(I)/2026 (Feb 2026, draft) would extend scope to services. Eight licensed integrators on FBR's list: Haball, WebDNAworks, EY Ford Rhodes, PRAL (free), OpenPort, TMR Consulting, NatureTech, Dynamic Resources. Minimum penalty Rs 500,000. Every local vendor sells "FBR-ready" as the headline feature, typically as a paid add-on (Rs 1,000–2,000/month/outlet).
- Provincial services-tax bodies (PRA, SRB, KPRA, BRA) each have their own POS/invoice reporting; vendors charge per-authority add-ons.

## 5. Market sizing and adoption evidence

- No credible published market-size or adoption-rate survey for accounting software among Pakistani SMEs was found. The 2026 Academicus paper (n=250 SME owners) models adoption drivers but reports no adoption percentage. SMEDA's SMAP figures are undated and ~2004–2010 era.
- Proxies: BSA 84% unlicensed rate (2015); US trade.gov puts the software sector at ~$3.2bn (Jan 2024) with no SME-tools breakdown; FBR's ~10% Tier-1 POS "connected" rate is the best hard number on real, sustained software use for tax purposes.
- Khata apps are the only products with mass-market proof (DigiKhata and Udhaar/Rupin each 5M+ installs). Everything double-entry is in the hundreds-to-low-thousands of paying customers per vendor (all self-reported).

## 6. What this means for a new open-source, PHP, self-hosted entrant

- FBR Digital Invoicing is table stakes, not a differentiator. Every local cloud vendor charges Rs 1,000–2,000/month for it. An open-source product that ships a working FBR DI/POS connector (PRAL is a free licensed integrator) removes the single recurring fee Pakistani SMEs resent most. The Manager.io thread shows exactly this demand.
- The paying local market is small and price-anchored at Rs 2,000–7,000/month. Vendors survive on add-on stacking. "Unlimited users, self-hosted, no add-ons" is a real message — but it monetises via hosting/support, not licences, and the total revenue pool is thin.
- Real competition is unlicensed QuickBooks/Peachtree on a desktop plus Excel, free khata apps, and the long tail of software-house PHP ERPs. Accountants are trained on QuickBooks/Peachtree/Tally, so import from those formats, familiar chart-of-accounts and report layouts, and a Jul–Jun fiscal year with Pakistani sales-tax/withholding logic matter more than novel UX.
- Offline/LAN-first is a feature, not a legacy. Mint POS, LookPOS and Candela sell offline capability against cloud-only rivals; a self-hosted PHP stack on a shop PC or cheap VPS fits Pakistani connectivity better than pure SaaS.
- Weak spots to exploit: Candela has no double-entry GL; Nimbus/khata apps have shallow accounting; Sidat Hyder/SowaanERP are enterprise-only; Odoo/ERPNext need Rs 300k+ implementation; long-tail PHP ERPs have no audit trail and vendor lock-in.
- Caveats: no vendor here has demonstrated a large paying base; Tier-1 compliance near 10% shows enforcement, not enthusiasm, drives purchases; open-source PHP alone earns nothing unless a reseller/implementer channel is cultivated.
