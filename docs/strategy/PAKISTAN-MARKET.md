# Pakistan market: what PHP Ledger is up against

Research recorded 15 September 2026 from public sources (FBR/PRAL documents, KPMG/EY alerts, Business Recorder, Dawn, Profit, ProPakistani, Google Play listings, vendor pricing pages). Figures are point-in-time; vendor claims are marked as such; anything not confirmed by a primary or reputable secondary source is marked UNVERIFIED. This complements [Pakistan tax research](../tax/PAKISTAN.md) and [Pakistan reporting research](../accounting/PAKISTAN_REPORTING_RESEARCH.md), which cover rules and statement formats; this document covers competitors, buyers and the compliance forcing functions that create demand. Nothing here claims a PHP Ledger capability. Full research behind each section: [vendors](research/pakistan-vendor-landscape.md), [FBR regime](research/pakistan-fbr-einvoicing-regime.md), [bookkeeping reality](research/pakistan-sme-bookkeeping-reality.md).

## 1. The market is three markets, not one

| Segment | Approximate size | What they use today | Legal pressure | Pays for software? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Informal shops and kiryana | 4–5 million retail outlets (est.); ~5.2M SMEs overall (Karandaaz 2023) | Paper register, WhatsApp, free khata apps (DigiKhata, Udhaar/Rupin, CreditBook, Easy Khata — each 5M+ installs) | Special Procedure for Small Shopkeepers TY2026: Rs 25,000 fixed tax, simplified return, explicitly **exempt from POS/digital invoicing** | No. DigiKhata Pro (Rs 200/month) reached ~100 installs in two years. |
| Sales-tax registered SMEs, sole traders, AOPs | ~400K traders filing returns; ~64K AOPs filing (FBR 2018); ~41K persons actually paying sales tax (2019 data) | Tax consultant at ~Rs 3,000/month; unlicensed desktop QuickBooks/Peachtree/Tally; Excel; local cloud tools | **Mandatory real-time FBR digital invoicing since 31 Dec 2025** for every sales-tax registered person; Finance Act 2026 (1 Jul 2026) allows suspension/blacklisting for non-integration | Yes, Rs 2,000–7,000/month tolerated; often the money goes to the consultant rather than software. |
| Pvt Ltd companies and Tier-1 retailers | ~199K SECP-registered companies; ~16–17K Tier-1 retail branches registered (only ~10% reported "connected" in Feb 2026) | Local ERPs (Candela, Sidat Hyder, Hisaab.pk, LedgerMax), Odoo/ERPNext via partners, SAP B1 at the top | Digital invoicing plus Tier-1 POS obligations; 60% input-tax disallowance and sealing for non-integration | Yes; Odoo partner implementations quoted at Rs 300K–3M. |

Only the second and third rows can be a paying market for an accounting system. The first row is served by free, VC-funded apps that monetise through easyload commissions, lending and payments, and every one that tried a subscription failed or pivoted.

### The long tail: software houses selling their own PHP ERP

Below the named vendors sits a much larger tier the listicles never capture: dozens to hundreds of local software houses selling a web ERP/POS under their own brand for a one-time fee plus annual maintenance — for example Sarwer Enterprise (erp1.sarwerent.com: "sales, stock, customers, purchases, accounts and reports", role-based access, 15+ interface languages), Efrotech, Gentec, Eccountant, ICT Systems, LogicLayer, Ohad Tech, Timeline Digi and SolutionDots, each publishing a "best ERP in Pakistan 2026" page that ranks itself first. Many of these products appear to be white-labelled or adapted commercial PHP scripts (the multilingual-by-default login page is characteristic of that origin) — UNVERIFIED for any specific vendor. Their economics: Rs 15,000–60,000 one-time, cPanel or shared hosting, no source escrow, and support that lasts as long as the house does. **This tier is PHP Ledger's most direct competitor on deployment and price**, and its weaknesses are precisely ours to exploit: no audit trail, no reviewed double-entry, no FBR integration beyond a bolt-on, and lock-in to a single small vendor. It is also the natural *reseller channel* for an open-source core — the same houses could ship PHP Ledger instead of a script they cannot maintain.

OraSoft: only a Karachi IT training institute (orasoft.edu.pk) was found under that name; the software vendor the owner refers to is UNVERIFIED pending a URL.

International products as seen in Pakistan: TallyPrime sold in USD by CIS (Silver ~USD 405 perpetual) with no Pakistan tax localisation; QuickBooks Desktop and Peachtree/Sage 50 dominate accountant training and job adverts despite no local sales channel (BSA measured 84% unlicensed software in Pakistan in 2015; no newer figure); Zoho Books has no PKR pricing page and no native FBR digital invoicing; Odoo lists ~60 "Ready"-grade partners for Pakistan; ERPNext has a handful of partners with "limited FBR integration options" (partner statement). Manager.io's forum shows Pakistani users of that free desktop tool asking for FBR digital invoicing and threatening to leave — evidence that free desktop tools have real Pakistani users who hit the compliance wall.

Every paid local vendor now sells "FBR-ready" as the headline and charges Rs 1,000–2,000 per month per outlet for it. Digital invoicing is therefore table stakes in the paying market, not a differentiator — but the recurring fee for it is the cost SMEs resent most.

## 3. The compliance forcing functions

- **Digital invoicing.** SRO 69(I)/2025 created the regime; SRO 1852(I)/2025 set the operative phase-in (largest taxpayers live 1 Nov 2025; all remaining registered persons live by 31 Dec 2025); STGO 01/2026 restated that all sales-tax registered persons must issue invoices through the FBR system via a licensed integrator, with a 72-hour edit/cancel window; Finance Act 2026 (effective 1 Jul 2026) added suspension/blacklisting for non-integration. Penalties under s.33 start at Rs 500,000. Invoices not issued through the system are invalid for the buyer's input tax.
- **Tier-1 retailer POS.** Definition unchanged (chains, malls, electricity bill above Rs 1.2M, card POS present, and so on). Finance Act 2026 folded the old "approved POS software" vocabulary into the licensed-integrator model. Non-integration: 60% input-tax disallowance, sealing.
- **Licensed integrators.** Eight on FBR's list as of September 2026: Haball, WebDNAworks, EY Ford Rhodes, PRAL, OpenPort, TMR Consulting, NatureTech, Dynamic Resources. PRAL must provide integration free on demand. Requirements to become one (Rules 150XH–150XO): Pakistan-incorporated company, paid-up capital of at least Rs 10 million, PASHA or ICAP registration certificate, three years' audited accounts, project list, documented ERP-integration capacity; licence valid five years, non-transferable; fee to taxpayers capped at Rs 10 per invoice or Rs 1 million per year. Application fee/bank guarantee: UNVERIFIED.
- **The API is public.** PRAL's Technical Specification for DI API v1.12 (July 2025) and User Manual v1.6 (April 2026) document a REST/JSON gateway at `gw.fbr.gov.pk` with sandbox endpoints, 28 test scenarios, bearer tokens issued through IRIS, IP whitelisting, and a required QR/logo on printed invoices. A software vendor does not need a licence to ship a client; the taxpayer needs an integrator of record (PRAL is free). Whether PRAL will whitelist many small self-hosted IPs at scale is a practical question: UNVERIFIED.
- **Returns.** Monthly STR-7 with Annex-A/B/C/D/F/H/I; Annex-C is now auto-populated from digital invoices, and reconciling ledger to DI data is the monthly pain point. 90% input-tax cap (s.8B). Withholding as agent under s.153/149/155 with periodic statements. Finance Act 2026 requires records in machine-readable form (CSV/XLSX/XML/JSON). Four provincial services-tax authorities (PRA, SRB, KPRA, BRA) each have their own returns and POS systems; FBR's draft SRO 288(I)/2026 to pull services into federal e-invoicing was opposed by the provinces and remains draft as of the latest verifiable source.
- **Income tax for traders.** Annual return plus wealth statement on IRIS (due 30 Sep 2026 for individuals/AOPs). Finance Act 2025 disallowances (cash sales above Rs 200,000; payments to persons without NTN) and s.114C non-filer restrictions push traders toward books. Tajir Dost was dropped in March 2025 after registering ~70–75K shopkeepers; the TY2026 small-shopkeeper special procedure replaced it.
- **No FBR-approved accounting software list exists.** "FBR-approved" in vendor marketing means sandbox scenarios passed or an integrator partnership. IRIS itself offers a manual invoice-entry mode for low-volume taxpayers — the real competitor at the bottom of the registered segment.

## 4. Who actually buys, and on what device

- 12% of households own a computer (PSLM 2019-20, latest found); 71.6% smartphone usage; 28% of mobile users still on 2G. No data exists on SME hosting ownership; it is negligible outside software firms and agencies. The shopkeeper has a phone and a JazzCash/Easypaisa QR; the only computer in the chain sits with the accountant.
- The tax consultant is the operator of the books for most registered SMEs (published fees: ~Rs 3,000/month for the sales-tax return, ~Rs 5,000/year for the business income-tax return). A system a consultant can run for 20–50 client companies has a buyer; a system aimed at the individual shop competes with five free apps.
- Khata apps: DigiKhata 5M+ installs, 210K reviews, 4.4★; Udhaar Book (rebranded Rupin) 5M+; CreditBook 5M+ but pivoted to licensed lending; Easy Khata 5M+ but still a pure ledger. Data Darbar (Nov 2022) counted 17.8M combined downloads against ~740K combined monthly actives (~4%). Installs are not usage and usage is not revenue.
- Payments: 2.03M merchants accept digital payments (Jun 2026) against 4–5M outlets; JazzCash Business has 10M+ installs with statements and payment requests but no ledger. Statement import and reconciliation is the accountant's manual monthly job today.
- The B2B retail-tech wave of 2021 (Bazaar, Retailo, Jugnu, Dastgyr, Tajir) is largely dead or pivoted; none sustained a business on software revenue from retailers.

## 5. What this means for PHP Ledger

1. **Target the registered tier: SMEs, sole traders, AOPs and Pvt Ltd companies that must e-invoice.** That is a market of a few hundred thousand entities with a legal obligation, an accountant, and a demonstrated tolerance for Rs 2,000–7,000/month. The informal tier is not a software market and the free apps own it.
2. **The buyer is usually the accountant or software house, not the owner.** Multi-company access, per-client permissions, consultant-friendly exports (Annex-A/C/I, withholding statements as XLSX/CSV) and a reseller-style install are what convert. "Runs on a simple VPS" describes the accountant's or reseller's capability, not the trader's.
3. **Ship an open-source FBR digital-invoicing client.** The API is public and sandboxed; PRAL is a free integrator of record; no licence is needed to ship the client. Nobody in the open-source category has one. Removing the Rs 1,000–2,000/month "FBR add-on" fee is the sharpest message available against every local vendor.
4. **Annex-C reconciliation and the 90% input-tax cap are recurring monthly pain that cheap local tools handle badly.** Building Annex-A/C/I from the ledger plus DI responses is a concrete, defensible feature.
5. **Becoming a licensed integrator is a separate business decision** with concrete entry requirements (Rs 10M paid-up capital, PASHA/ICAP certificate, audited accounts). It is a services line for the sponsoring company, not a prerequisite for the product.
6. **Design for the counter on a phone and the month-end on a desktop.** Every product with mass adoption is Android-first with Urdu UI and offline entry; every product accountants trust is desktop. A mobile web entry path and an Urdu catalog are adoption features in this market, not polish.
7. **Do not build a second, hidden set of books.** The informal "kacha khata" (unposted udhaar/credit tracking, cash memos, drafts) has a legitimate product form — a sub-ledger that is visibly not yet posted, like the khata apps — but a mode whose purpose is to keep transactions out of the statutory ledger is incompatible with accountant sign-off, with FBR licensing, and with Finance Act 2026 enforcement. The existing roadmap's rule that multi-book must mean *legitimate alternative reporting bases* stands.

## 6. Open questions to verify before committing

- PRAL's practical willingness to whitelist many small self-hosted installations (or whether a hosted relay by a licensed integrator is the realistic path).
- Final status of SRO 288(I)/2026 (services e-invoicing) and the small-shopkeeper turnover threshold (sources conflict between ~Rs 20M and Rs 200M).
- Any newer measurement of accounting-software adoption among Pakistani SMEs (none found after the 2015 BSA figure and undated SMEDA numbers).
