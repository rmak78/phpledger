# Multi-Currency, FX, and Group Consolidation

_Approved by the owner, 15 September 2026. Implementation reference for the AR/AP milestone and the group reporting layer._

> **Schema dependency — read first.** §1 must be implemented **before the AR/AP schemas are finalised**. Multi-currency adds columns to every journal line and a property to every account. Retrofitting it after AR/AP ships is a migration across the entire ledger. The *features* in §§4–8 can follow later; the *storage shape* in §1 cannot. §8.4 carries the same warning for intercompany tagging.

This sits alongside immutable journals and period locking as accountant-grade correctness (competitive landscape, gap 4). The Tier-3 invoicing tools — Invoice Ninja, InvoicePlane, Crater — implement a currency field on the invoice and nothing else: no revaluation, no realised/unrealised split. Akaunting's is thin. None of them consolidate. This is checked in the first ten minutes of an accountant's evaluation.

---

## 1. Non-negotiable storage shape

### 1.1 Every journal line

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `currency` | Transaction currency (ISO 4217) |
| `amount_fc` | Amount in transaction currency |
| `rate` | Rate applied at posting |
| `rate_type` | Which rate was used (§5) |
| `rate_source_id` | FK to the rate row used; nullable for manual |
| `amount_base` | Amount in the book's functional currency |
| `rate_is_stale` | Flag — the rate used was carried forward from an earlier date (§6.4) |
| `ic_counterparty_entity_id` | Intercompany counterparty, nullable (§8.4) |

Never store only `amount_base` (the real FC balance is lost) and never only `amount_fc` (no trial balance possible). Both, always, even for domestic lines where `currency = base` and `rate = 1`. A uniform shape is worth the storage.

**The rate is frozen at posting.** Updating the rate table must never restate a posted journal. Same principle as reversal-not-edit.

### 1.2 Every account

- `currency` — nullable. `NULL` means the account accepts any currency (most P&L accounts, AR/AP control). A value means the account is denominated in that currency (§3).
- `is_monetary` — drives revaluation (§4.3).
- `revaluation_account_id` — where this account's unrealised difference posts.
- `group_account_id` — mapping to the group chart of accounts (§8.3).

### 1.3 Every company/book

- `functional_currency` — the books' currency. Set at creation, **immutable thereafter**. Changing it is a new book plus a migration, never a settings edit.
- `presentation_currency` — reporting only; defaults to functional.
- `group_id`, `parent_entity_id`, `ownership_pct`, `consolidation_method` — see §8.1.

## 2. Three currencies, distinguished

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional** | The currency the books are kept in | PKR for a Pakistani entity |
| **Transaction** | The currency a given document is denominated in | A USD 2,000 export invoice |
| **Presentation** | The currency a report is rendered in | Consolidated group reporting in PKR |

A book has one functional currency and unlimited transaction currencies. Do not conflate "we deal in USD" with "our books are in USD."

---

## 3. Currency is an account property

A USD bank account **is** a USD account. It holds a USD balance that is a fact of the world, plus a base-currency carrying value that floats.

Foreign-currency accounts therefore carry a **dual balance**:
- FC balance — real, never touched by revaluation
- Base carrying value — derived, adjusted by revaluation

Posting a non-matching currency to a designated account is rejected. You cannot put EUR into a USD bank account; that is two transactions with a conversion between them.

AR and AP control accounts stay currency-agnostic; the sub-ledger holds currency per open item, and revaluation runs **per open item**, not on the control-account total.

## 4. Realised and unrealised FX

### 4.1 Realised — on settlement

Arises when the rate at settlement differs from the rate at recognition. Posts to P&L on the settlement date.

**Always prefer the actual rate the bank gave you** over any published rate. The bank's rate is fact; the market rate is an estimate. The settlement UI must accept an actual-rate override, and when given it, that rate is authoritative.

### 4.2 Unrealised — at period end

Open FC monetary balances are retranslated at the closing rate. The difference posts to P&L (single entity, per IAS 21).

**Design: post the revaluation dated period-end and auto-reverse it on day one of the next period.** Each period's revaluation is then a single self-contained, auditable pair, and differences never compound. The reversal must be generated by the same run, not left to the user.

Revaluation must be re-runnable: re-running for a period reverses the prior run and posts fresh. Never stack.

### 4.3 Monetary vs non-monetary — the distinction most software gets wrong

| Retranslated at closing rate | Held at historical rate, never retranslated |
|---|---|
| Cash and bank balances | Fixed assets |
| Receivables and payables | Inventory at cost |
| Loans and advances | Prepayments |
| Accrued income and expenses | Capital contributed by the owner |
| | Gold and commodity holdings (§7) |

Getting this wrong produces an owner's equity figure that drifts for no reason — which directly damages the real-time equity feature (A3). Equity contributed in a foreign currency stays at the rate on the day it was contributed, permanently.

## 5. Rate types

One rate is not enough.

| Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `spot` | Default for posting |
| `actual` | The realised bank rate — overrides spot whenever known |
| `closing` | Period-end revaluation and balance-sheet translation |
| `average` | P&L translation in consolidation (§8) |
| `tax` | FBR/Customs conversion for import valuation and foreign-currency invoices — **not** the market rate |
| `documentary` | The rate on a Proceeds Realisation Certificate or E-Form |

**`tax` is locally specific and mandatory.** Converting a foreign-currency purchase at the market rate for sales-tax purposes is wrong. This rate type must exist before the FBR DI client posts anything involving foreign currency, and it is a distinct lookup from `spot`.

**`documentary` serves the IT-export and freelancer segment.** When export proceeds land through the banking channel, the PRC rate is the documentary rate for tax purposes. Support attaching the certificate as evidence on the settlement and using its rate in place of spot. Small feature, large audience — Pakistan's freelance and IT-export base is a natural first constituency for this product.

## 6. Rate table and providers

### 6.1 Schema

```
currency_rates (
  id, from_currency, to_currency, rate_date, rate_type,
  rate, source, fetched_at, entered_by, note
)
UNIQUE (from_currency, to_currency, rate_date, rate_type, source)
```

**Append-only.** Corrections insert a new row; they never update an existing one, because posted journals reference the row that was there at the time. Store rates at full precision (minimum 8 decimal places) and round only at presentation.

### 6.2 Providers — pluggable, same pattern as the tax adapter

1. **Manual entry** — always available, works with no network. The baseline.
2. **CSV import** — bulk history, and the accountant's actual workflow.
3. **SBP published rates** — the authoritative PKR source.
4. **Commercial API** — for installs that want daily automation.

Note for planning: the free ECB-derived feeds do not publish PKR. PKR sourcing means SBP or a paid API. Do not promise daily automatic rates in marketing copy before this is settled.

### 6.3 Hosting-floor constraints

1. **Manual entry always works.** A cPanel install may have no outbound HTTP and an unreliable cron. The fetch is an enhancement, never a dependency.
2. **Posting never blocks on a missing rate.** Prompt for it inline; let the user proceed.
3. **Fetch runs on the same cron-driven queue as outbound events** (parties doc §5.4). No synchronous HTTP in a request path.

### 6.4 Missing-rate rules

- No rate for date D → use the most recent rate **before** D and set `rate_is_stale`.
- **Never interpolate.** A fabricated rate is worse than a stale one because it looks precise.
- **Never reach forward** to a later rate. That is backdating with extra steps, and it breaks period locking.
- A report listing entries posted on stale rates is part of the period-close checklist.

## 7. Gold and commodity holdings

**Gold is not a currency.** Modelling it as one implies debts can be settled in it at a spot rate and produces the wrong tax treatment — gain on gold is a capital gain, not an FX gain.

Model it as a **quantity-denominated asset**:
- Account holds a quantity with a unit (tola, gram, ounce) and a base-currency cost basis
- Non-monetary — the period-end FX run skips it entirely
- Gain or loss recognised **on disposal**, against cost basis
- Optional mark-to-market through a revaluation reserve, off by default

Quantity-plus-cost-basis is the same machinery the inventory module needs. Build it once; gold, inventory and any commodity use it. Not a special case, and not a reason to delay — but it belongs after the core FX layer.

## 8. Group structures, consolidation and combined reporting

The requirement: a parent with up to three (or more) subsidiaries must produce combined financial statements at each closing. Entities may sit in different countries and different functional currencies — Pakistan and UAE first (A7).

### 8.1 Group model

Each company/book carries:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `group_id` | Which reporting group it belongs to |
| `parent_entity_id` | Nullable; null for the top entity or for sister entities under common control |
| `ownership_pct` | Parent's holding |
| `consolidation_method` | `full` / `equity` / `combined` / `excluded` |

Method selection:
- **Full** — control, normally >50%. Line-by-line aggregation, with non-controlling interest recognised where holding <100% (§8.7).
- **Equity method** — significant influence, roughly 20–50%. Single-line investment carried at cost plus share of post-acquisition profit. No line-by-line aggregation.
- **Combined** — entities under **common control with no parent-subsidiary relationship**. Aggregated without a holding company, no NCI, intercompany still eliminated.
- **Excluded** — in the group register but outside this report.

**The `combined` case is not an edge case.** Sister entities under common ownership — the Bixisoft / Agency75 / Bixitech pattern — are the most common Pakistani SMB group shape, and it is exactly what "combined financial reports" usually means here. Most software assumes a holding company exists. Support both from the start; the difference is whether NCI and investment elimination apply, not whether aggregation happens.

### 8.2 Translation follows different rules from transaction FX

| Item | Rate | Difference goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Balance sheet | Closing | — |
| P&L | Average for the period | — |
| Resulting imbalance | — | **Translation reserve in equity, not P&L** |

This is the critical divergence. Transaction FX differences hit profit; translation of a foreign operation does not. If translation differences leak into P&L, both consolidated profit and the headline equity figure are wrong, and an accountant spots it immediately.

The translation reserve is tracked **per entity**, so the group balance sheet can show where each component came from.

Because of this, consolidation must be designed alongside the equity feature (A3), not bolted on afterwards.

### 8.3 Chart of accounts alignment

Each entity keeps its own local chart of accounts — a UAE entity's chart will not match a Pakistani one. Consolidation runs on a **group chart of accounts**, with every local account mapped via `group_account_id` (§1.2).

- Mapping is many-to-one: several local accounts may roll into one group account.
- An unmapped account with a non-zero balance **blocks the group close** and is listed in an exception report. Silently dropping it produces an out-of-balance consolidation that nobody can trace.
- The seeded chart ships pre-mapped so single-entity users never see this.

### 8.4 Intercompany tagging — schema-level, do it now

`ic_counterparty_entity_id` on the journal line (§1.1) is the second thing in this document that cannot be retrofitted. Without it, elimination becomes manual archaeology through descriptions and reference numbers.

Tag at source:
- Sales and purchases between group entities
- Intercompany receivables and payables
- Intercompany loans and the FX arising on them
- Management fees, cost recharges, royalties
- Dividends from a subsidiary to the parent

The UI sets this automatically when the counterparty party record is linked to a group entity — which is why the party model (parties doc §2) must carry an optional link to a group entity.

### 8.5 Eliminations

Run at group close, in this order:

1. **Intercompany balances** — AR against AP, loans against borrowings
2. **Intercompany trading** — revenue against cost of sales
3. **Unrealised profit in inventory** — group profit on stock still held inside the group at period end
4. **Intercompany dividends** — against the parent's income
5. **Investment in subsidiary against subsidiary equity** — full method only; not applicable to combined
6. **FX on intercompany monetary items** — these do not net to zero when the two entities have different functional currencies; the residual is a real group-level difference and must be visible, not squashed

**Reciprocity check runs before elimination.** Intercompany balances must agree between the two books. Any mismatch is reported per pair with both amounts and the difference, and the group close is blocked until it is explained or accepted with a reason. Mismatches are normal — timing differences and in-transit goods — but they must be surfaced, never auto-plugged.

### 8.6 Consolidation entries live in their own layer

Elimination and translation entries post to a **consolidation layer belonging to the group**, never into a subsidiary's own ledger. A subsidiary's statutory books must remain exactly what it files with its own authority.

The layer is: re-runnable, fully reversible, dated to the group period, and attributed to the run that produced it. Re-running a group close reverses the prior run's entries and posts fresh.

### 8.7 Non-controlling interests

Where the parent holds less than 100% under the full method, NCI is presented separately in both equity and profit for the period. Computed from `ownership_pct` at the entity level. Sub-subsidiaries multiply through the chain.

### 8.8 Close sequencing

Group close is gated. In order:

1. Every included entity completes its own period close, including FX revaluation (§4.2), and **locks the period**
2. Rates for the group period are present: `closing` and `average` for each functional currency (§5)
3. Chart mapping is complete (§8.3)
4. Reciprocity check passes or is accepted with reasons (§8.5)
5. Group close runs; consolidation layer is written
6. Group period locks

A group close that runs against an unlocked subsidiary period is invalid the moment that subsidiary posts another entry. Enforce this — do not warn.

### 8.9 Output format

The deliverable accountants expect is a **consolidation worksheet**: one column per entity, then eliminations, then the group total, reconciling across. Give them that, plus the consolidated P&L, balance sheet and equity statement.

Also useful and nearly free once the layer exists: per-entity comparative columns, and a group-level AR/AP aging with intercompany shown separately from third-party. The latter answers "how much does the group actually owe outsiders," which is the number an owner wants.

### 8.10 Practical notes

- **AED is pegged to USD at 3.6725.** AED/USD is effectively fixed; only PKR floats against both. This simplifies test fixtures — an AED/PKR rate can be derived from USD/PKR.
- **Goodwill on acquisition is out of scope** for the first pass. Most SMB groups here are formed by common ownership, not acquisition. Record the exclusion explicitly rather than half-implementing it.
- Entities may have different financial year-ends. First pass: require a common period for consolidation and say so plainly. Handling non-coterminous year-ends is a later problem.

## 9. Reporting requirements

- **Trial balance** — always functional currency.
- **AR/AP aging** — available in both invoice currency and base; group-level aging separates intercompany from third-party (§8.9).
- **Foreign currency balances report** — per FC account: FC balance, base carrying value, rate used, unrealised difference. This is the report a freelancer or exporter actually opens.
- **FX gain/loss summary** — realised and unrealised shown separately, never netted into one line.
- **Stale-rate exceptions** — part of period close (§6.4).
- **Consolidation worksheet** — per §8.9.
- Every report with monetary values carries an explicit currency indicator. No ambiguous number anywhere in the UI.

## 12. Worked cases for the test suite

These are the correctness fixtures. Volume testing proves nothing here; these do.

**Case 1 — realised FX on an export invoice**
5 Jan: invoice USD 2,000 at spot 281.00 → Dr AR (USD 2,000 / PKR 562,000), Cr Revenue PKR 562,000.
20 Feb: bank credits at actual rate 279.50 → PKR 559,000.
Expected: Dr Bank 559,000; Dr Realised FX loss 3,000; Cr AR (USD 2,000 / PKR 562,000). AR closes to zero in **both** currencies.

**Case 2 — unrealised revaluation, auto-reversed**
USD bank: FC balance USD 4,200, carrying value PKR 1,176,000 (historical 280.00).
31 Jan closing rate 283.00 → 4,200 × 283 = PKR 1,188,600.
Expected: Dr USD Bank (base only) 12,600; Cr Unrealised FX gain 12,600. **FC balance stays USD 4,200.** Entry reverses 1 Feb.

**Case 3 — non-monetary not retranslated**
Laptop bought for USD 1,500 at 279.00 → PKR 418,500.
At closing rate 283.00, the asset remains PKR 418,500. Depreciation computes on PKR 418,500. No FX entry. Assert the revaluation run produces **nothing** for this account.

**Case 4 — gold on disposal**
10 tola at PKR 240,000/tola → cost basis PKR 2,400,000, quantity 10 tola.
Period-end FX run: no entry.
Sell 4 tola at PKR 265,000 → proceeds 1,060,000, cost relieved 960,000, gain PKR 100,000 to **capital gain**, not FX gain. Remaining: 6 tola, PKR 1,440,000.

**Case 5 — equity contributed in foreign currency**
Owner contributes USD 10,000 at 275.00 → PKR 2,750,000 in capital.
At every subsequent closing rate, capital remains PKR 2,750,000. Assert no revaluation ever touches it.

**Case 6 — intercompany elimination, same currency**
Parent (PKR) invoices Subsidiary A (PKR) PKR 500,000 for management fees. Both sides tagged.
Consolidated: revenue and expense both eliminated; group P&L impact zero. Parent AR and Subsidiary AP both eliminated; group balance sheet unchanged in total.

**Case 7 — intercompany elimination, cross-currency**
Parent (PKR) lends Subsidiary B (AED) the equivalent of AED 100,000.
Assert: the two legs do **not** net to zero after translation, the residual lands where §8.5 item 6 specifies, and it is visible on the worksheet rather than absorbed silently.

**Case 8 — reciprocity mismatch blocks close**
Parent books an IC receivable of PKR 300,000; Subsidiary A books an IC payable of PKR 280,000.
Assert: group close is blocked, the pair is reported with both amounts and the PKR 20,000 difference, and no auto-plug entry is created.

**Case 9 — combined reporting, no parent**
Three sister entities under common control, no holding company, all `combined`.
Assert: line-by-line aggregation occurs, intercompany is eliminated, **no NCI is computed**, and **no investment-against-equity elimination** is attempted.

**Case 10 — non-controlling interest**
Parent holds 70% of Subsidiary C under the full method.
Assert: 100% of assets, liabilities and P&L are aggregated; 30% of net assets and 30% of profit are presented separately as NCI.

**Case 11 — consolidation does not touch statutory books**
Run a group close, then reverse it.
Assert: no journal in any subsidiary's own ledger changed at any point, and the subsidiary trial balance is byte-identical before and after.

**Case 12 — group close gating**
Attempt a group close while one subsidiary's period is unlocked.
Assert: refused, not warned. Then lock it, close successfully, and assert the group period locks.

**Case 13 — stale rate**
Post on a date with no rate. Assert: most recent prior rate used, `rate_is_stale` set, entry appears in the stale-rate exception report, and no rate dated after the posting date is ever selected.

**Case 14 — rate change does not restate history**
Post an entry, then insert a corrected rate for that same date. Assert the posted journal is unchanged in both amount and rate.

**Case 15 — unmapped account blocks group close**
Give a subsidiary an account with a balance and no `group_account_id`.
Assert: group close is blocked and the account appears in the mapping exception report. It is never silently dropped.
