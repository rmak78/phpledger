Complete double-entry accounting that runs on your own PHP hosting

PHP Ledger records invoices, bills, stock movements and bank transactions as balanced journals, then shows the profit and loss, balance sheet and cash position behind them. It is open source under AGPL, installed from your browser on PHP 8.2+ and MySQL 8.4. Version 1.0.0 is the first stable release.

In short

PHP Ledger is open-source, self-hosted double-entry accounting software for small businesses, built on PHP and MySQL, with a cash point of sale that can be switched on when a counter needs it.

  • Version 1.0.0 includes invoices, bills, allocated payments, credits, ageing, weighted-average stock, bank reconciliation and financial reports on PHP 8.2+ and MySQL 8.4 hosting.
  • Installation runs in the browser at /install; signed automatic updates make a matched backup first and recover automatically if a migration fails.
  • The public demo gives each visitor a private company from eleven fictional business histories or a blank playground, reset every hour.
  • Regional tax certification, e-invoicing, a production shop POS, payroll, multi-currency posting and translations are not in 1.0; the roadmap lists them as later releases.

Why small businesses choose PHP Ledger

The whole accounting core is open source

Invoices, bills, stock, bank reconciliation and reports work without a paid app to open any of them. The AGPL keeps every module in the open, so an accountant or a software house can read exactly how a figure was posted. A commercial licence is available for organisations that need to embed or distribute the code without AGPL obligations.

It runs on the hosting you already have

PHP 8.2+ and MySQL 8.4 run on shared hosting or a small server that a shop in Multan or Manchester can rent for a few dollars a month. Install from the browser in minutes, then update with signed releases that make an automatic backup before they touch a file, and recover automatically if a migration fails.

Every figure is traceable

Reports drill down to journals and from journals to the source document, so a profit and loss line can be explained to the person who asks. Posted entries are never edited: a correction is a linked reversal with its reason recorded, and a draft never touches the books until someone reviews and posts it.

It says what it has not done yet

Published limits, published test results and a roadmap with review gates instead of dates. Regional tax certification, a production shop POS, payroll and multi-currency posting are listed as not yet done, and independent accounting and security reviews are open commitments, so you can see what is ready and what still needs review.

A tour of the main screens

Invoices and credits keep the register, the status and the open amount in view

Use the Draft, Unpaid, Overdue, Paid and Reversed filters in the register to see who owes what. The selected invoice shows its due date, whether prices were entered with or without tax, the reference and the remaining balance, and a receipt can be recorded without leaving the list. Customer credits sit in the same register, so a partly paid invoice and the credit that settles it are never far apart.

See sales in the product tour

Profit and loss with gross profit, on the period you choose

Choose a calendar preset or your own dates. Income, cost of sales, gross profit and expenses appear in one report, and every account name opens its statement so a surprising total can be traced to the entries behind it. The report exports to CSV for your accountant, and the balance sheet, trial balance and ageing reports follow the same period controls.

See the reports

Import a bank statement, match it, keep the history

Paste or upload a CSV statement and preview it before anything posts. Matching suggestions are confirmed explicitly, one line at a time, so an unexplained bank movement is investigated rather than absorbed. Each completed statement stays with the account, which makes the next reconciliation start from a known opening balance instead of a guess.

See banking in the product tour

Products and stock with weighted-average cost of sales

Keep stock and non-stock products in one register with the quantity on hand and the carrying value side by side. Stock counts preview their accounting effect before they post, goods receipts and returns move quantities through the same books, and the cost of sales appears on the profit and loss without a separate spreadsheet.

See inventory in the product tour

A cash counter that posts through the same books

Build the cart, review the cash and change, print the receipt. A confirmed sale records one balanced journal through the central posting service, so the day's takings appear in the reports without re-keying. The module is optional and can be switched off for businesses that sell through invoices alone; stock, tax and card payments at the counter are planned for a later release.

Questions before you start

Short answers to the questions owners and accountants ask first. Each one links to the page with the detail.

Is PHP Ledger really free?

Yes. The project-owned core is free under AGPL-3.0-or-later, and every module ships with it: invoices, bills, stock, bank reconciliation and reports. Hosting, setup help, optional support and a separate commercial licence have their own terms. The 0.1.0 through 0.1.5 previews retain their earlier MIT grants.

What do I need to run it?

PHP 8.2 or newer for both web and command-line requests, MySQL 8.4 and HTTPS. Installation happens in the browser at /install, so no terminal access, Composer, Node or Docker is needed for setup. PHP 8.3 is recommended, and the PHP zip extension is needed for signed automatic updates.

Does it work on shared cPanel hosting?

Often, but a panel alone does not prove it. Your plan must provide the required PHP extensions, MySQL 8.4, private files and the correct public document root. Run the browser installer's checks on the actual account before you commit to it.

Does PHP Ledger send my data anywhere?

No. There are no licence-key or licensing-server calls, and this website has no analytics or form submission endpoint. The installed application has one documented optional country lookup; every connection is explicit and scoped. The privacy page explains the separate website, demo and installed-application boundaries.

Can it invoice customers and settle supplier bills?

Yes. Version 1.0 includes customer invoices, supplier bills, partial and final payments allocated across open items, linked credits, ageing and control-account reconciliation. Accounts receivable and payable belong to the core, and owners can hide navigation they do not use.

Is it ready for real books?

1.0.0 is the first stable release, backed by automated tests, fault-injection tests of the update and recovery path, and exact-artifact installation and upgrade checks. Independent accounting review, independent security review and supervised pilots have not happened yet; they are open post-release commitments. Use a fictional company until you have completed your own reviewed acceptance.

What if the project stops?

You keep the source and your self-hosted database under the applicable licence. Continued maintenance, recovery and data extraction still need an operator, so keep tested backups and portable report exports rather than relying on a ZIP alone.
